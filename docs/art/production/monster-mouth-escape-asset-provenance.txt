Post-processing, the same for every asset, with per-asset values in assets.json; pixelize.py process:
1. Crops the rectangle given. A seamless tile cross-fades the last quarter of its crop into the first along each tiling axis and drops it, so its edges meet.
2. Masks the background: the given key colour connected to the border (or every pixel near it, for "enclosed"), or the alpha of a background-removed source; optionally keeps only the largest connected figure.
3. Applies the asset's recolour rules, if any: hue ranges moved to red (tomato, meat, full heart, vending machine) or desaturated to grey steel (UI frames, slot, plain button, clock case); the empty heart takes the full heart's crop desaturated and darkened; the dead neon signs are desaturated and darkened; the overpass pillars move from magenta to indigo; and the version 5 walkway's slate stone moves to violet.
4. Detects the generation's own pixel grid from the spectrum of its column and row edges. Where both axes show a grid at least 4 times the median spectral strength, it moves the crop onto that grid so each output pixel is one drawn block, or a whole number of blocks when the asset is drawn coarser than the generation, so the crop still covers the figure; otherwise it divides the crop evenly. Ground tiles that join the road skip detection, so their deck rows stay level with the road's. manifest.json does not record which; process prints it.
5. Takes one colour per block: the most frequent master-palette colour in the block's central half; a block is clear when most of it is background.
6. Limits the asset's colours to a subset of the 85-colour master palette in assets.json (64 median-cut from the three concept images, the 15 swatches of the two character sheets, and 6 greys from the concept's UI panels): 16 for characters, 12 for every world, walkway, ground and decoration sprite and for icons and UI, 32 for backgrounds, workshop and key art, 16 for the logo. The most used colour is kept first, then each next pick maximises its use times its distance from the colours already kept, so small accents survive. Every other block takes its nearest kept colour.
7. Cleans: clears opaque pixels with at most one opaque 4-neighbour, fills clear pixels with three or more, and replaces a pixel that touches none of its own colour by the colour around it when the two are close, so contrasting single pixels such as eyes stay. Then merges lone pixels, of a colour none of their 8 neighbours share, into the colour most common around them, keeping only the most contrasting ones, at most 0.4% of the opaque pixels.
8. Icons: sets every edge pixel to the outline colour #0a060e, giving a 1 px dark outline. Characters, world props, walkway pieces, ground and decorations ("edge": "lit") take the one selective outline lit from above instead: an edge facing up shows the colour under it, and every other edge facing clear space takes the asset's own colour nearest 45% of the darker of itself and the pixel inside it. A tile's border faces nothing.
8a. Pieces that join another ("match" in assets.json): the colour limit of step 6 first keeps every colour of the piece joined, and after step 8 each row takes its outer columns on each joined side, the cut varying by row, aligned to the bottom or, with "top", the top. Any two such pieces side by side then meet without a seam.
8b. Merges lone pixels again, as in step 7, since the outline and the joins leave some.
9. Enlarges by the asset's scale with nearest-neighbour. Every pixel is fully opaque or fully clear.
Raw generations are not committed. Running pixelize.py fetch and process again writes the committed PNGs and manifest.json byte for byte. pixelize.py check then measures the clean-art rules of project/levels/monster_mouth_escape/art/README.md on every PNG in the manifest.
