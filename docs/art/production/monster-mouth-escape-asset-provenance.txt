Monster-mouth escape asset provenance, 2026-11-02, issues #90 and #98

Version 3, issue #98: adds the ground tiles and decorations, and fixes the noodle vendor and the empty heart handed over from the review of PR #96. Version 2 was the set after that review. Characters are cut from the approved concept sheets with no generation. Every other asset comes from Recraft, through its MCP connector, personal account; two ground sources are Recraft image_edit results, described under Ground. Every generation used the custom style below; Recraft ran it on model recraftv4_1_raster. No seed is exposed. Recraft returns WebP; the source URL of each generation is kept in tools/art/assets.json.
Tools: Python 3.13.5 runs tools/art/pixelize.py, standard library only. ImageMagick 7.1.1-43 Q16 x86_64 decodes WebP in pixelize.py fetch (PNG24, or PNG32 for background-removed sources).
Post-processing, the same for every asset, with per-asset values in assets.json; pixelize.py process:
1. Crops the rectangle given. A seamless tile cross-fades the last quarter of its crop into the first along each tiling axis and drops it, so its edges meet.
2. Masks the background: the given key colour connected to the border (or every pixel near it, for "enclosed"), or the alpha of a background-removed source; optionally keeps only the largest connected figure.
3. Applies the asset's recolour rules, if any: hue ranges moved to red (tomato, meat, full heart, vending machine) or desaturated to grey steel (UI frames, slot, plain button, clock case); the empty heart takes the full heart's crop desaturated and darkened; the dead neon signs are desaturated and darkened; the overpass pillars move from magenta to indigo.
4. Detects the generation's own pixel grid from the spectrum of its column and row edges. Where both axes show a grid at least 4 times the median spectral strength, it moves the crop onto that grid so each output pixel is one drawn block, or a whole number of blocks when the asset is drawn coarser than the generation, so the crop still covers the figure; otherwise it divides the crop evenly. Ground tiles that join the road skip detection, so their deck rows stay level with the road's. manifest.json does not record which; process prints it.
5. Takes one colour per block: the most frequent master-palette colour in the block's central half; a block is clear when most of it is background.
6. Limits the asset's colours to a subset of the 85-colour master palette in assets.json (64 median-cut from the three concept images, the 15 swatches of the two character sheets, and 6 greys from the concept's UI panels): 16 for characters, 12 for icons and UI, 24 for world tiles and props, 16 for the intact road and the plank bridge, 20 to 24 for the other ground tiles, 24 for the train cars, facades and rubble piles, 16 for the other decorations, 32 for backgrounds, workshop and key art, 16 for the logo. The most used colour is kept first, then each next pick maximises its use times its distance from the colours already kept, so small accents survive. Every other block takes its nearest kept colour.
7. Cleans: clears opaque pixels with at most one opaque 4-neighbour, fills clear pixels with three or more, and replaces a pixel that touches none of its own colour by the colour around it when the two are close, so contrasting single pixels such as eyes stay.
8. Characters and icons: sets every edge pixel to the outline colour #0a060e, giving a 1 px dark outline. World props, ground tiles and decorations have none.
8a. Ground tiles that join the road ("match" in assets.json): the colour limit of step 6 first keeps every colour of ground/road.png, and after step 7 each row takes the road's outer 4 to 8 columns on each joined side, the cut varying by row. Any two such tiles side by side then meet as road meets road, which tiles without a seam.
9. Enlarges by the asset's scale with nearest-neighbour. Every pixel is fully opaque or fully clear.
Raw generations are not committed. Running pixelize.py fetch and process again writes the committed PNGs and manifest.json byte for byte.
