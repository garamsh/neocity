# Presentation

How the GDExtension presents the title screen and the survival game through input and a drawn frame.

## Current decisions

- The `neocity` GDExtension library is built from `src/register_types.cpp`, `src/scene.cpp`, and the session, input, rendering, HUD, and audio folders. It depends on the `survival` library's public surface and godot-cpp 10.0.0-stable. The root `build_profile.json` limits the generated bindings to the Godot classes it uses.
- `src/register_types.cpp` is the extension entry point (`neocity_init`) and registers `MainScene`.
- `src/scene.cpp` (`MainScene`, the root of `project/main.tscn`) is the composition site and frame adapter: it constructs the session, drawing, the `audio::Speaker`, and one `SurvivalRules` per survival run, and forwards frame input and drawing to session. It provides session's `SoundStage` from the speaker, and stops the speaker as it leaves the tree. It exposes `screen_name()`, `survival_status()`, `sound_status()`, what the speaker played and plays now, `display_status()`, the display choice in force and whether it was saved, `pin_display_size()`, which stands in for the display's size, `pin_survival_seed()`, `pin_scene_clock()`, which holds `Art`'s clock, on which the scene's ambient motion, the `Finish` shader and every value `Art` eases run, where a check puts it, and the static `controls_keys()`, the controls page's rows, for the Godot checks; an unpinned run takes its seed from Godot's random generator. It also provides session's `CinematicStage`: it loads a cinematic scene as its child, closes it on the `finished` signal, and reports a scene that cannot play instead of aborting. It starts each frame on `Art` before anything in it is eased or drawn. It draws on four canvas items, back to front: a `World` child, a `Glow` child and a `Finish` child, all drawn behind it, and itself. `World` takes session's scene, `Glow` its light through the blend-add material `project/levels/monster_mouth_escape/art/glow.tres`, `Finish` the pass that finishes both through the shader material `project/levels/monster_mouth_escape/art/finish.tres`, and `MainScene` the menus, the map's words and the interface. Session reads the run's frame once per process, after it advances, reports that frame through `survival_status()`, and works out once what that frame shows, the `rendering::Shot`: the camera as it eases, where it heads, the sources out of reach and each human's goal. Every layer draws that frame through that shot. `World` is named so the Godot checks can read the meta the backdrop leaves on it. Each layer's material is read back after it is set, and a layer that did not take it stops the host.
- `src/session/` owns selection and run orchestration as described in [session](session.md).
- `src/input/` translates Godot input actions into menu actions and the survival input of each human actor. It reads where the mouse is from the current scene's canvas and binds the left button to the `click` action. `project.godot` binds the menu actions `menu_up`, `menu_down`, `confirm`, `menu`, `pause`, and `option_1` to `option_4`, and the `survival_1_*` and `survival_2_*` actions. The jump keys are Space for the first set and `;` for the second, which sits beside L and collides with no other key in use. `menu_left` and `menu_right`, A or left and D or right, change a volume, a resolution or the window mode on the pause screen, and the marked row of the Settings page.
- `src/rendering/` loads the native city environment and city actors from `project/levels/monster_mouth_escape/art_hd/`, and the kitchen, interface, icons and other existing resources from `project/levels/monster_mouth_escape/art/` and the interface font from `project/levels/monster_mouth_escape/fonts/`, and owns the shared drawing primitives and the colour tokens. The font is Tiny5, a pixel face on a grid of 8 pixels per em, under the SIL Open Font License, whose text sits beside it, imported with no antialiasing, no hinting and no subpixel positioning; [its provenance](../art/production/monster-mouth-escape-font-provenance.txt) records the source. The canvas is 1920×1080 at the window's real output resolution, as [ADR 0024](adr/0024-render-a-1920x1080-canvas-at-the-output-resolution.md) decides; `src/rendering/screen.hpp` holds its size and the art's scale of 1.5, and every layout below is still written in the 1280×720 units that scale takes onto it. `src/rendering/display.cpp` keeps the window's resolution and mode, and `src/hud/display_rows.cpp` draws and targets their rows, with a back and a forward arrow each, the resolution's arrows refused with a reason in full screen, where its value is the display's size, and a window's list stopped at the display's size, on the pause screen under the volumes and on the Settings page, the title's fifth entry, which has no number key. `rendering::Art` draws words at the whole multiple of that grid nearest the size asked, with each baseline on a whole pixel, so every pixel of the face is a square of whole layout units, which the canvas shows at `ART_SCALE` canvas pixels each; Bold draws the words again one pixel to the right. `Art` also keeps the lines it wrote, a line continued where words are drawn on its baseline at its end, and the size each word was drawn at after the grid, until they are taken; the session leaves them on the canvas as the `screen_text` and `screen_text_sizes` metas, and `project/e2e/survival.gd` checks on the base, the city and the pause screen that every drawn size is a multiple of the grid, 16 at the least, and that the font is imported without antialiasing. The two lines share `Companion`'s search, which runs the physics step movement uses. The map's marks search the same rule from each human: from where they stand in the city, from the entrance at base.
- `src/session/sound.cpp` turns what a frame of play did into cues: footsteps every cell and a half walked on the ground, a jump, a landing, and the commands the rules carried out, burns, falls, a new day and rising acid, which it reads from the run's events and from its views before and after the frame. A refused command has no cue. `src/audio/` names the cues, the tracks and the two buses, and `src/audio/speaker.cpp` plays them through one Godot `AudioStreamPlayer` per cue on the Effects bus, and the music on the Music bus: the title music on the menus, the workshop's at base and the city's in the city, whose pitch rises with the acid and over which a drone grows louder with it; a cinematic and a finished run play none. The sounds are the CC0 files of `project/levels/monster_mouth_escape/audio/`, recorded in the [sound provenance](../art/production/monster-mouth-escape-sound-provenance.txt). Sound reads the rules and moves nothing in them.
- `src/rendering/feedback.cpp` draws the flourishes of the same cues for a moment of real time, which session ages while the run is not held: rings and specks where a collection happened, acid thrown up where an actor fell in and along the whole surface when the acid rises, steam over the pot when a dish is cooked or burned, the monster's YUM! or BLEH! over its bucket, and a fade through dark when a new day starts. They show whatever the volumes.
- Godot provides window, input, resource, audio, and canvas primitives. No custom GPU backend is implemented.
