# Presentation

How the GDExtension presents the title screen and the survival game through input and a drawn frame.

## Current decisions

- The `neocity` GDExtension library is built from `src/register_types.cpp`, `src/scene.cpp`, and the session, input, rendering, and HUD folders. It depends on the `survival` library's public surface and godot-cpp 10.0.0-stable. The root `build_profile.json` limits the generated bindings to the Godot classes it uses.
- `src/register_types.cpp` is the extension entry point (`neocity_init`) and registers `MainScene`.
- `src/scene.cpp` (`MainScene`, the root of `project/main.tscn`) is the composition site and frame adapter: it constructs the session, drawing, and one `SurvivalRules` per survival run, and forwards frame input and drawing to session. It exposes `screen_name()`, `survival_status()`, and `pin_survival_seed()` for the Godot checks; an unpinned run takes its seed from Godot's random generator. It also provides session's `CinematicStage`: it loads a cinematic scene as its child, closes it on the `finished` signal, and reports a scene that cannot play instead of aborting.
- `src/session/` owns selection and run orchestration as described in [session](session.md).
- `src/input/` translates Godot input actions into menu actions and the survival input of each human actor. `project.godot` binds the menu actions `menu_up`, `menu_down`, `confirm`, `menu`, `pause`, and `option_1` to `option_3`, and the `survival_1_*` and `survival_2_*` actions.
- `src/rendering/` loads the monster-mouth escape art set from `project/levels/monster_mouth_escape/art/` and Godot's fallback font, and owns the shared drawing primitives and color palette. `rendering::Art` draws each picture at its own size or a whole multiple of it, on whole pixels, and the project's default nearest texture filter keeps the pixel art sharp. `src/rendering/survival.cpp` paints the survival game: the throat, the distant city and the acid sea, whose surface rises with acid from below the map's lowest ground row, so every ground row shows at acid 0; then the city grid in layers, back to front: backdrop scenery, buildings and shop fronts, the ground tile of every cell of standing terrain, the acid drip and passages, hanging and standing scenery, people and sources, and the actors with their labels. Destroyed terrain, buildings, passages and people each have a mark, and a broken passage is named. A gold frame promises stock a player can walk to: stock no human can reach has a dashed white frame and says so in its label, so the difference is not colour alone. Every word on the map sits on an opaque plate, so the acid behind it cannot change its contrast, and each source's count is painted over the actors. Each actor stands on its cell's floor and rises above it, faces the way it last walked, alternates two walking frames for a moment after each step, and uses its climbing frame on a ladder; `Art` remembers each actor's last cell and world time between frames for this. The workshop shows the kitchen with both actors and the monster's feeding bucket.
- `src/hud/` paints the interface with the rendering primitives. `src/hud/menus.cpp` draws the title screen over the monster's mouth, marking the cursor's entry, and the controls page, which lists both players' keys from its own table, as the pause screen does from its own. `src/hud/survival.cpp` draws the choice of players and the survival HUD. The survival HUD reads one `SurvivalFrame` of snapshots and shows the day with the day escape checks begin, read from `TEMPORARY_ESCAPE` with no chance shown, acid against the rules' full value, time, each actor's controller, health, stamina, location and tools, the pack, the base tool states, the recipes with their taste, a marker on the dish the chef's key would cook and that dish's forecast from `dish_score`, stored dishes with their quality and score, the ingredients eaten so far, the last day's result over the kitchen, which reads snapshots and so holds no clock and waits for no key, an event log clear of the monster's bowl, and a NEXT line naming what to do now, with the time a collection spends there and in the key line. Text is 13px or larger. `changing_text` lists each line that changes with play, the cook panel's status in every form a pack could give it, with the width its place allows, so `project/e2e/survival.gd` can measure them. Under a cinematic it paints the dark stage, or the placeholder card when no scene can play, as the [cinematic contract](session.md#cinematic-contract) describes.
- The NEXT line asks `SurvivalRun`'s offers what the human's key would do with the current view, so the offer it names is the command the key runs.
- A stored food the monster rejected cannot be fed, so the feed key does nothing with it. At base the NEXT line runs in this order: appraise, replace a damaged tool, cook when no stored dish can be fed, set out, feed, end the day unfed. Set out is named only while `survival::worth_entering` says a human has an errand from the entrance, because feeding ends the day and a stored dish keeps; with two humans it names both set-out keys, `[Q]/[U]`, and either player's errand counts. While an AI chef has a dish it would cook and no stored dish can be fed, it says so, because that chef cooks on its next step. With nothing to do it names Feed, or ending the day unfed when no stored dish can be fed, whatever time is left, since ending the day adds its unspent time to world time, and world time is what brings the next bag. In the city it names the object AI in the human's place would walk to and the direction of the first step, and a collection, with its time cost, only where AI would stop, so a source passed on the way to what the party lacks does not spend the clock; the interact key still takes from it, and still repairs a passage the line leaves out. With two humans in the city it gives each player's line, and while one is in the city it tells the other, at base, to set out whenever that player has an errand from the entrance. The key line names the travel key as setting out for a human at base and as returning for one in the city. It says nothing is within reach when no stocked source, or passage that actor can repair to open the way to stock, can be walked to. The two lines judge the same human by the same search, so following them cannot circle; a partner's errand is not counted, so a human leaves an AI partner's repair undone by going home. The two lines share `Companion`'s search, which uses the public cell rule movement uses. The map's marks search the same rule from each human: from where they stand in the city, from the entrance at base.
- Godot provides window, input, resource, and canvas primitives. No custom GPU backend is implemented.
