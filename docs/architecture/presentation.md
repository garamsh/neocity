# Presentation

How the GDExtension presents the title screen and the survival game through input and a drawn frame.

## Current decisions

- The `neocity` GDExtension library is built from `src/register_types.cpp`, `src/scene.cpp`, and the session, input, rendering, and HUD folders. It depends on the `survival` library's public surface and godot-cpp 10.0.0-stable. The root `build_profile.json` limits the generated bindings to the Godot classes it uses.
- `src/register_types.cpp` is the extension entry point (`neocity_init`) and registers `MainScene`.
- `src/scene.cpp` (`MainScene`, the root of `project/main.tscn`) is the composition site and frame adapter: it constructs the session, drawing, and one `SurvivalRules` per survival run, and forwards frame input and drawing to session. It exposes `screen_name()`, `survival_status()`, `pin_survival_seed()`, and the static `controls_keys()`, the controls page's rows, for the Godot checks; an unpinned run takes its seed from Godot's random generator. It also provides session's `CinematicStage`: it loads a cinematic scene as its child, closes it on the `finished` signal, and reports a scene that cannot play instead of aborting.
- `src/session/` owns selection and run orchestration as described in [session](session.md).
- `src/input/` translates Godot input actions into menu actions and the survival input of each human actor. `project.godot` binds the menu actions `menu_up`, `menu_down`, `confirm`, `menu`, `pause`, and `option_1` to `option_3`, and the `survival_1_*` and `survival_2_*` actions. The jump keys are Space for the first set and `;` for the second, which sits beside L and collides with no other key in use.
- `src/rendering/` loads the monster-mouth escape art set from `project/levels/monster_mouth_escape/art/` and the interface font from `project/levels/monster_mouth_escape/fonts/`, and owns the shared drawing primitives and the colour tokens. The font is Inter 4.1 in three weights, Medium, SemiBold and Bold, under the SIL Open Font License, whose text sits beside it; [its provenance](../art/production/monster-mouth-escape-font-provenance.txt) records the source. The interface is flat and drawn in code, with no frame pictures: a panel is an opaque rounded rectangle with a one-pixel edge and a soft shadow below it, an idle button a raised rounded rectangle with the same edge, and a marked button the accent with a darker lip along its bottom. `rendering::Art` eases a value it is asked to show from its last value toward a new one, and tells how far a part of the screen has come in since it appeared, on a steady real clock that moves nothing but the drawing; a value missing from the last drawn frame starts afresh. `rendering::Art` draws each picture at its own size or a whole multiple of it, on whole pixels, its colours multiplied by a tint where one is given, and the project's default nearest texture filter keeps the pixel art sharp. `src/rendering/survival.cpp` paints the survival game: the throat, the distant city and the acid sea, whose surface rises with acid from below the map's lowest ground row, so every ground row shows at acid 0; then the city grid in layers, back to front: backdrop scenery, the supports under the walkways, buildings and shop fronts, the walkway of every cell of standing terrain, the kiosk wreck on the road it blocks, the acid drip and passages, hanging and standing scenery, people and sources, the actors, and every label. The throat, the city and the backdrop scenery are tinted darker and cooler than the play layer, and the acid fades from the glow of its surface to a dark colour below it, so it does not flood the screen behind a high sea. Each walkway hangs under its cell's floor line, where an actor's feet are: a deck on an X-braced truss, its plates varied along a row; a jagged torn end where the road is broken; a ramp where a step rises to the right, stairs where it rises to the left, and a deck where it is level; and rubble or a wrecked car standing on its deck. A support, a scaffold, an I-beam column or a concrete pillar in turn from one run of walkway to the next, stands under each end of a run and every fourth column between, down to the walkway below or into the acid. The walkways are drawn brighter than their own colours, and their walked-on top edge brightest, so the walkable surface reads first at every acid level. The buildings and terrain `Content::landmarks` names show their own pictures: the kiosk wreck, the cold store, which is the front of the cold locker's shop inside it, the ferry's hull, the pier's iron deck, and the rotten bridge's frame under its planks. Destroyed terrain, buildings, passages and people each have a mark, and a broken passage is named. A gold frame promises stock a player can walk to: stock no human can reach has a dashed white frame and says so in its label, so the difference is not colour alone. Every word on the map sits on an opaque plate over the art, so the acid behind it cannot change its contrast. No plate covers another or a source: each source's count takes its own cell first, then the other words, and the actors' tags last, each at the spot it asks for or the nearest free one. The city leaves the plates it drew last as the canvas's `map_plates` meta, so `project/e2e/survival.gd` and `project/e2e/survival_capture.gd` can measure them. Each actor is drawn at its feet's sub-cell position, which `Art` eases toward each new one so it moves smoothly between steps; it faces the way it last moved. On the ground it shows its landing frame for 120ms of world time after it comes down, then, for a moment after each sideways move, two running frames when that move was faster than halfway from walking to running speed and four walking frames otherwise, each frame advancing with the distance covered so the feet keep pace with the ground; in the air its rising or its falling frame; and on a ladder its climbing frame, mirrored every half cell it climbs so its reaching arm alternates. `Art` remembers each actor's last sideways position and stance, with their world times, between frames for this. The workshop shows the kitchen with both actors and the monster's feeding bucket.
- The colour tokens, from `rendering::palette()`, which the map's labels use too:

- `src/hud/` paints the interface with the rendering primitives, the interface font, and the art set's pixel-art hearts, top-band icons, and ingredient, tool, and dish icons, which it finds by the display names the `survival` library gives. Titles and numbers are Bold, every line that changes with play is SemiBold, and fixed notes are Medium; the day, the acid, the time, NEXT, panel titles, buttons, and the names and scores of ready, offered, and stored dishes are 18px or larger, and other text is 13px or larger and sits on an opaque panel, button, chip, or plate. `src/hud/menus.cpp` draws the title screen, the key art and the logo with a panel per entry and the accent highlight sliding to the cursor's entry, and the controls page. The controls page's rows are the pause screen's rows for two players followed by the pause and leave keys, built by the same function from the same key table, so the two cannot name different keys. `src/hud/survival.cpp` draws the choice of players, a panel per choice with the same sliding highlight, and the survival HUD. The survival HUD reads one `SurvivalFrame` of snapshots and shows the day with the day escape checks begin, read from `TEMPORARY_ESCAPE` with no chance shown, acid against the rules' full value, time, each actor's controller, health, stamina, location and tools, the pack, the base tool states, the recipes with their taste, the dish the chef's key would cook and that dish's forecast from `dish_score`, stored dishes with their quality and score, the ingredients eaten so far, the last day's result over the kitchen, which reads snapshots and so holds no clock and waits for no key, an event log, and a NEXT line naming what to do now. Cards along the top edge show the day, the acid with its gauge, the time, Esc and M with what they open, and, at the right end, the log, newest line brightest, clear of the kitchen's actors, pot and bowl. In the city, cards below the labels of the map's lowest row show a card per actor, with its portrait on a tile, its place, hearts, a stamina gauge, its base tool, and its spare tools as icons with counts; the pack as ingredient icons with counts, a mark on each one not yet identified, and the time each collection spends; and the action card. The action card gives a lone human its interact key as a large button, lit while the key acts now and named for what it does there, Collect or Repair, with what it acts on beside it, or Use when it acts on nothing; under it the travel key, Return to base or Set out, lit when NEXT asks for it, and the move and run keys. Two humans each get a row of the same two buttons. Every key stays on the pause and controls pages, which the top band's Esc card names. At base the appraise panel shows the pack on tiles, the one the appraiser's key would appraise ringed; the cook panel the dish the chef's key would cook in a wide row with its icon and forecast, the other recipes that can be cooked now large in two columns, and the rest small with what they lack; and the feed panel each stored dish as its icon with name and score, the score counting up as it appears, over a large feed button. Each panel's keys sit on buttons, lit while they act now. The workshop's panels rise into place as the workshop opens, and the pause and leave screens fade their veil in and rise. The acid and stamina gauges ease to each new value. A hand-off notice takes the NEXT bar's second line. `changing_text` lists each line that changes with play, the cook panel's status in every form a pack could give it, the action card's labels, and the top band's Esc and M notes, with the width its place allows, so `project/e2e/survival.gd` can measure them in SemiBold. Under a cinematic it paints the dark stage, or the placeholder card when no scene can play, as the [cinematic contract](session.md#cinematic-contract) describes.
- The NEXT line asks `SurvivalRun`'s offers what the human's key would do with the current view, so the offer it names is the command the key runs.
- A stored food the monster rejected cannot be fed, so the feed key does nothing with it. At base the NEXT line runs in this order: appraise, replace a damaged tool, cook when no stored dish can be fed, set out, feed, end the day unfed. Set out is named only while `survival::worth_entering` says a human has an errand from the entrance, because feeding ends the day and a stored dish keeps; with two humans it names both set-out keys, `[Q]/[U]`, and either player's errand counts. While an AI chef has a dish it would cook and no stored dish can be fed, it says so, because that chef cooks on its next step. With nothing to do it names Feed, or ending the day unfed when no stored dish can be fed, whatever time is left, since ending the day adds its unspent time to world time, and world time is what brings the next bag. In the city it names the object AI in the human's place would go to and the first move: walk or jump left or right, the jump with its key, or climb up or down; in the air, the move from where the drop lands. It names a collection, with its time cost, only where AI would stop, so a source passed on the way to what the party lacks does not spend the clock, and a repair passed on the way only where `survival::repair_opens_stock` says it brings stock into reach; the interact key still takes from the source, and still repairs a passage the line leaves out. With two humans in the city it gives each player's line, and while one is in the city it tells the other, at base, to set out whenever that player has an errand from the entrance. The key line names the travel key as setting out for a human at base and as returning for one in the city. It says nothing is within reach when no stocked source, or passage that actor can repair to open the way to stock, can be walked to. The two lines judge the same human by the same search, so following them cannot circle; a partner's errand is not counted, so a human leaves an AI partner's repair undone by going home. The two lines share `Companion`'s search, which runs the physics step movement uses. The map's marks search the same rule from each human: from where they stand in the city, from the entrance at base.
- Godot provides window, input, resource, and canvas primitives. No custom GPU backend is implemented.
