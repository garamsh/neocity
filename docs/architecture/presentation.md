# Presentation

How the GDExtension presents the title screen and the survival game through input and a drawn frame.

## Current decisions

- The `neocity` GDExtension library is built from `src/register_types.cpp`, `src/scene.cpp`, and the session, input, rendering, HUD, and audio folders. It depends on the `survival` library's public surface and godot-cpp 10.0.0-stable. The root `build_profile.json` limits the generated bindings to the Godot classes it uses.
- `src/register_types.cpp` is the extension entry point (`neocity_init`) and registers `MainScene`.
- `src/scene.cpp` (`MainScene`, the root of `project/main.tscn`) is the composition site and frame adapter: it constructs the session, drawing, the `audio::Speaker`, and one `SurvivalRules` per survival run, and forwards frame input and drawing to session. It provides session's `SoundStage` from the speaker, and stops the speaker as it leaves the tree. It exposes `screen_name()`, `survival_status()`, `sound_status()`, what the speaker played and plays now, `pin_survival_seed()`, and the static `controls_keys()`, the controls page's rows, for the Godot checks; an unpinned run takes its seed from Godot's random generator. It also provides session's `CinematicStage`: it loads a cinematic scene as its child, closes it on the `finished` signal, and reports a scene that cannot play instead of aborting. It draws on three canvas items, back to front: a `World` child and a `Glow` child, both drawn behind it, and itself. `World` takes session's scene, `Glow` its light through the blend-add material `project/levels/monster_mouth_escape/art/glow.tres`, and `MainScene` the menus, the map's words and the interface. Session reads the run's frame once per process, after it advances, reports that frame through `survival_status()`, and works out once what that frame shows, the `rendering::Shot`: the camera as it eases, where it heads, the sources out of reach and each human's goal. Every layer draws that frame through that shot. `World` is named so the Godot checks can read the meta the backdrop leaves on it. The glow layer's material is read back after it is set, and a layer that did not take it stops the host.
- `src/session/` owns selection and run orchestration as described in [session](session.md).
- `src/input/` translates Godot input actions into menu actions and the survival input of each human actor. It reads where the mouse is from the current scene's canvas and binds the left button to the `click` action. `project.godot` binds the menu actions `menu_up`, `menu_down`, `confirm`, `menu`, `pause`, and `option_1` to `option_3`, and the `survival_1_*` and `survival_2_*` actions. The jump keys are Space for the first set and `;` for the second, which sits beside L and collides with no other key in use. `menu_left` and `menu_right`, A or left and D or right, change a volume on the pause screen.
- `src/rendering/` loads the monster-mouth escape art set from `project/levels/monster_mouth_escape/art/` and the interface font from `project/levels/monster_mouth_escape/fonts/`, and owns the shared drawing primitives and the colour tokens. The font is Tiny5, a pixel face on a grid of 8 pixels per em, under the SIL Open Font License, whose text sits beside it, imported with no antialiasing, no hinting and no subpixel positioning; [its provenance](../art/production/monster-mouth-escape-font-provenance.txt) records the source. `rendering::Art` draws words at the whole multiple of that grid nearest the size asked, with each baseline on a whole pixel, so every pixel of the face is a square of whole screen pixels; Bold draws the words again one pixel to the right. `Art` also keeps the lines it wrote, a line continued where words are drawn on its baseline at its end, and the size each word was drawn at after the grid, until they are taken; the session leaves them on the canvas as the `screen_text` and `screen_text_sizes` metas, and `project/e2e/survival.gd` checks on the base, the city and the pause screen that every drawn size is a multiple of the grid, 16 at the least, and that the font is imported without antialiasing. The interface is drawn in pixel panels in code, on whole pixels, as [ADR 0010](adr/0010-frame-the-hud-in-the-corners-like-the-concept.md) decides: a panel is a dark fill inside a thick light rim, its lower half in shade, between dark lines, with cut corners; an idle button the same frame on a raised fill, a marked button the accent inside a light gold rim with a darker lip along its bottom, and a slot or trough a dark inset. `rendering::place_name` gives the one name the screen uses for a city object: a source by what it holds and what it is, such as Fish cart or Wrench bag, and anything else by the `survival` library's name. `rendering::Art` eases a value it is asked to show from its last value toward a new one, and tells how far a part of the screen has come in since it appeared and how long it has been shown, on a steady real clock that moves nothing but the drawing; a value missing from the last drawn frame starts afresh. `rendering::Art` draws each picture at its own size or a whole multiple of it, on whole pixels, its colours multiplied by a tint where one is given, and the project's default nearest texture filter keeps the pixel art sharp; `Art::look` draws what follows through a camera's scale and offset. `src/rendering/camera.cpp` is the city's camera, as [ADR 0009](adr/0009-follow-the-humans-with-a-close-camera.md) decides: `aim` works out where it heads and `follow` eases it there; it maps the scene, the 1280×720 pixels the map sits in at its old place, to the screen, its centre drawn at the middle of the play area between the top bar and the cards. It follows the humans in the city and never an AI partner, easing on `Art`'s real clock: one human at twice the scene's size, 80 pixels ahead of the way it faces; two framed together, at a zoom between once and twice that shrinks as they part. With no human in the city it rests on the base entrance. It never shows past the map's sides or the scene's top and bottom, and centres on the map when the view is wider than it. At base the whole scene is shown at its own size. `src/rendering/survival.cpp` paints the survival game. The backdrop is three layers drawn at their own size, which slide sideways, and the middle one also up and down, a part of the way the camera moves, the farther the less, each beside mirrored copies of itself so it covers the screen: the throat with its palate, teeth and uvula, raised so they hang over the city, its near-white parts pulled toward its magenta; the swallowed city of tilted towers sunk in flesh with an elevated railway and a skewered train, its towers below the teeth and its base under the acid band; and an acid band of sunken trains and broken pillars in its own glowing colours, its surface 70 pixels above the old bottom of the play area at acid 0, which rises with acid. Then, through the camera, the acid sea, whose surface rises with acid from below the map's lowest ground row, so every ground row shows at acid 0; then the city grid in layers, back to front: backdrop scenery, the supports, buildings and shop fronts, the walkway of every cell of standing terrain, the kiosk wreck on the road it blocks, the acid drip and passages, hanging and standing scenery, people and sources, the actors, and every label. No haze is laid over the backdrop: as [ADR 0011](adr/0011-open-the-city-to-a-bright-throat-over-glowing-acid.md) decides, the screen's values run from the dark walkways through the bright throat and city to the acid, brightest; the backdrop scenery in the map is drawn in its own colours; each acid sea fades from the glow of its surface, the band to a slightly less bright green below it and the sea in the scene to a deep green. On the glow layer, which adds its light to the scene, each acid surface sends up light, and each street lamp and acid drip has a halo. Each walkway hangs under its cell's floor line, where an actor's feet are: a thick stone deck, its blocks varied along a row, eaten by the acid once the acid reaches it, and with flesh dripping over the front edge of a few; a jagged torn end where the road is broken; a ramp where a step rises to the right, stairs cut from the deck in steps where it rises to the left, and a worn deck where it is level; a fallen slab, where tilted ground runs along a row, drawn as one worn deck tilted from the floor line of its high end down to the row below at its low end; and rubble or a wrecked car standing on its deck. Nothing fills the space under the walkways, so the backdrop shows between them. A support, a concrete pillar or an I-beam column in turn by run, stands under each end of every run of walkway deck and of the pier, but the planks, the tilted ground and the other landmarks' terrain, and under every fourth column between, and a concrete pillar under each end of a shop front's footing of deck, down to the walkway below or into the acid. The walkways and the supports are drawn darker than their own colours, but for the face under the stairs' steps, which keeps its own, under a walked-on top edge lit warm and brightest, drawn from the deck picture's rows under its dark top outline, or from the top rows of the pier's picture, which has none, along the tilt on a ramp, stairs or fallen slab, so the walkable surface reads first over the brighter backdrop at every acid level. The buildings and terrain `Content::landmarks` names show their own pictures: the kiosk wreck, the cold store, which is the front of the cold locker's shop inside it, the ferry's hull, the pier's iron deck, and the rotten bridge's frame under its planks. Destroyed terrain, buildings, passages and people each have a mark, and a broken passage is named. A gold frame promises stock a player can walk to: stock no human can reach has a dashed white frame and says so in its label, so the difference is not colour alone. The map's words are drawn on the screen, not through the camera, at their own size where the camera shows what they name, and words for what is off the play area are left out. Every word on the map sits on an opaque plate over the art, so the acid behind it cannot change its contrast. A bag that is empty or carried off is not drawn, since a later bag may land on its cell; every other source keeps showing its count, empty or destroyed. A goal the NEXT line names for a human, when wholly off the play area, gets a pointer inside the area's edge: an arrow aimed at it on a dark disc, with its name. No plate covers another, a pointer, or a source other than its own count: each source's count takes its spot first, in its own cell, then the pointers' names, then the other words, and the actors' tags last, each at the spot it asks for or the nearest free one. The city leaves on the canvas the plates it drew last as the `map_plates` meta, the names of the sources it drew as `map_sources`, their areas on the screen as `map_source_areas`, the names of those whose count is on the play area as `map_labelled`, the names it points at as `map_pointers`, the camera as `map_view` and where it heads as `map_heading`, each a `Transform2D` from the scene to the screen; the backdrop leaves where it drew each layer, back to front, as the `backdrop_layers` meta on `World`, so `project/e2e/survival.gd` and `project/e2e/survival_capture.gd` can measure them. Each actor is drawn at its feet's sub-cell position, which `Art` eases toward each new one so it moves smoothly between steps; it faces the way it last moved. On the ground it shows its landing frame for 120ms of world time after it comes down, then, for a moment after each sideways move, two running frames when that move was faster than halfway from walking to running speed and four walking frames otherwise, each frame advancing with the distance covered so the feet keep pace with the ground; in the air its rising or its falling frame; and on a ladder its climbing frame, mirrored every half cell it climbs so its reaching arm alternates. `Art` remembers each actor's last sideways position and stance, with their world times, between frames for this. The workshop shows the kitchen with both actors and the monster's feeding bucket.

- `src/hud/pointer.cpp` holds what a click acts on, `hud::Target`, and lights the target under the mouse, writing a refusing target's reason over it. It leaves the targets on the main canvas as the `pointer_targets` meta for the Godot checks. Each screen's painting and its targets in `src/hud/` share one source for every area.
- `src/hud/` paints the interface with the rendering primitives, the interface font, and the art set's pixel-art hearts, corner icons, and ingredient, tool, and dish icons, which it finds by the display names the `survival` library gives. Corner numbers, titles and key caps are Bold and every other word is Regular; no word is smaller than 16px, two screen pixels to each of the face's, and every word sits on an opaque panel, button, slot, chip, plate or note. `src/hud/menus.cpp` draws the title screen, the key art and the logo with a panel per entry and the accent highlight sliding to the cursor's entry, and the controls page. The controls page's rows are the pause screen's rows for two players followed by the pause and leave keys, built by the same function from the same key table, so the two cannot name different keys. `src/hud/survival.cpp` draws the choice of players, a panel per choice with the same sliding highlight, and the survival HUD. Its `shot_of` asks the camera what to show, in the city, with the sources out of reach and each human's NEXT goal to point at, and the city, its light and its words are drawn from that shot. The survival HUD reads one `SurvivalFrame` of snapshots and keeps to the corners, as ADR 0010 decides. At the top, 48 pixels tall: the day, with a sun, at the left, its number two digits in the accent; the acid as a percentage of the rules' full value with a gauge in the middle; and the time left, minutes and seconds of two digits, with a clock at the right, with the Esc key's hint under it. In the city the goal line under the acid is the NEXT line, sized to its words, with a hand-off notice under it. The log shows as notes at the right edge under the Esc hint, newest first, each fading out four seconds after it appears; each event is told apart by its number in the session's log, so a line the log repeats appears afresh. Along the bottom, 84 pixels tall: each actor's face from its idle frame in a framed slot, who plays it and whether it is in the city or at base, a damaged base tool, its hearts and its stamina in five segments; the bag, numbered slots holding each ingredient the party carries and each actor's spare tools as an icon and count, a mark on each ingredient not yet identified and on each spare tool the player tag of who carries it, since a repair or a replacement takes the acting actor's own; more kinds than slots share the bag's width, each slot's marks drawn over the next slot; the HUD leaves the drawn items on the canvas as the `bag` meta; and in the city the action. The action gives a lone human its interact key as one large button, lit while the key acts now and named for what it does there and the screen name of what it acts on, such as COLLECT Fish cart or REPAIR Roof Ladder, or USE when it acts on nothing, or, while a collection holds the actor, the time the hold has left, and under it that human's move, run, jump and travel keys, travel left out while a collection holds the actor. Two humans each get a row of their player tag and the same button in their own key over their own keys, in an action panel 100 pixels tall. Over the head of each human in the city whose interact key acts now, above the map's tag, a key cap names that human's key; the HUD leaves those keys on the canvas as the `head_caps` meta, and `action_labels` gives what each action button reads, which session reports with each object's screen name, so `project/e2e/survival.gd` can read both. At base the kitchen shows the ingredients not yet identified over the appraiser's head, the one the appraiser's key would appraise ringed; one task card above the bottom row names the NEXT step large, then what the offered dish would score from `dish_score`, plain and fine, against the threshold, or, while a stored dish can be fed, its feed key unless the step names it and what feeding asks, then how many recipes can be cooked now with the recipe book's key, the choice keys when the chef can choose, and the end-day key with its acid; and the bottom right shows the stored dishes with their scores, which count up as they appear, gold once they pass, under the score the monster needs. The last day's result shows above the task card, clear of the appraiser's ingredients and the notes; it reads snapshots and so holds no clock and waits for no key. The pause screen holds every key of the current number of players, the recipe book, each recipe with what it still needs or that it is ready, the monster's mark and its taste, the ingredients eaten so far and what feeding does, when the escape checks begin, and the effects and music volumes, each ten blocks and a percentage, the one the left and right keys change marked in the accent. The workshop's task card rises into place as the workshop opens, and the pause and leave screens fade their veil in and rise. The acid gauge and the stamina segments ease to each new value. `changing_text` lists each line that changes with play, with the width its place allows, the recipe book's needs in every form a pack could give them, the hold's words on each button, and the widest form of each actor's top line, so `project/e2e/survival.gd` can measure them in the face; every sentence the log can show is listed too. `session` leaves on the main canvas the lines `Art` wrote for it in a frame as the `screen_text` meta, so the checks read what the screen drew. Under a cinematic it paints the dark stage, or the placeholder card when no scene can play, as the [cinematic contract](session.md#cinematic-contract) describes.
- The NEXT line asks `SurvivalRun`'s offers what the human's key would do with the current view, so the offer it names is the command the key runs.
- A stored food the monster rejected cannot be fed, so the feed key does nothing with it. At base the NEXT line runs in this order: appraise, replace a damaged tool, cook when no stored dish can be fed, set out, feed, end the day unfed. Set out is named only while `survival::worth_entering` says a human has an errand from the entrance, because feeding ends the day and a stored dish keeps; with two humans it names both set-out keys, `[Q]/[U]`, and either player's errand counts. While an AI chef has a dish it would cook and no stored dish can be fed, it says so, because that chef cooks on its next step. With nothing to do it names Feed, or ending the day unfed when no stored dish can be fed, whatever time is left, since ending the day adds its unspent time to world time, and world time is what brings the next bag. While a collection holds the human it names the source and the time left. Otherwise in the city it names the object AI in the human's place would go to and the first move: walk or jump left or right, the jump with its key, or climb up or down; in the air, the move from where the drop lands. It names a collection, with the time it holds the actor, only where AI would stop, so a source passed on the way to what the party lacks does not hold the human, and a repair passed on the way only where `survival::repair_opens_stock` says it brings stock into reach; the interact key still takes from the source, and still repairs a passage the line leaves out. With two humans in the city it gives each player's line, and while one is in the city it tells the other, at base, to set out whenever that player has an errand from the entrance. The key line names the travel key as setting out for a human at base and as returning for one in the city. It says nothing is within reach when no stocked source, or passage that actor can repair to open the way to stock, can be walked to. The two lines judge the same human by the same search, so following them cannot circle; a partner's errand is not counted, so a human leaves an AI partner's repair undone by going home. The two lines share `Companion`'s search, which runs the physics step movement uses. The map's marks search the same rule from each human: from where they stand in the city, from the entrance at base.
- `src/session/sound.cpp` turns what a frame of play did into cues: footsteps every cell and a half walked on the ground, a jump, a landing, and the commands the rules carried out, burns, falls, a new day and rising acid, which it reads from the run's events and from its views before and after the frame. A refused command has no cue. `src/audio/` names the cues, the tracks and the two buses, and `src/audio/speaker.cpp` plays them through one Godot `AudioStreamPlayer` per cue on the Effects bus, and the music on the Music bus: the title music on the menus, the workshop's at base and the city's in the city, whose pitch rises with the acid and over which a drone grows louder with it; a cinematic and a finished run play none. The sounds are the CC0 files of `project/levels/monster_mouth_escape/audio/`, recorded in the [sound provenance](../art/production/monster-mouth-escape-sound-provenance.txt). Sound reads the rules and moves nothing in them.
- `src/rendering/feedback.cpp` draws the flourishes of the same cues for a moment of real time, which session ages while the run is not held: rings and specks where a collection or a repair happened, a red ring on a burned actor with a red flash over the screen and a short shake of the world under the interface, acid thrown up where an actor fell in and along the whole surface when the acid rises, steam over the pot when a dish is cooked or burned, the monster's YUM! or BLEH! over its bucket, and a fade through dark when a new day starts. They show whatever the volumes.
- Godot provides window, input, resource, audio, and canvas primitives. No custom GPU backend is implemented.

## Rationale

[ADR 0003](adr/0003-implement-the-ascent-domain-in-cpp.md) selects godot-cpp for the adapter. [ADR 0004](adr/0004-adopt-the-godot-cpp-template-layout.md) splits it into one folder per presentation purpose with the entry point and composition site at the `src/` root. [ADR 0006](adr/0006-ship-one-game-behind-a-title-screen.md) removes the ascent prototype's camera and painting and the experiment hosting, and names the root `MainScene`. [ADR 0009](adr/0009-follow-the-humans-with-a-close-camera.md) replaces [ADR 0007](adr/0007-move-actors-through-a-side-view-with-gravity.md)'s one screen with a camera that follows the humans, and sets the backdrop's layers, haze and added light. [ADR 0011](adr/0011-open-the-city-to-a-bright-throat-over-glowing-acid.md) removes the haze and the walls under the walkways, so dark walkways on supports stand over a bright throat and a glowing acid band. [ADR 0010](adr/0010-frame-the-hud-in-the-corners-like-the-concept.md) replaces the flat interface of [#107](https://github.com/garamsh/neocity/issues/107) with pixel panels in the corners, a pixel font, a goal line and details on the pause screen.
